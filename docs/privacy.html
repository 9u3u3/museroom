MUSEROOM

Privacy

Last updated 3 September 2026

Museroom shows what you are listening to, shares it with friends you add, plays what they are playing, finds listeners near you, and counts your minutes. This page says exactly what it reads, what it keeps, and what other people can see.

What it reads on your phone

Android publishes a media session for whichever app is playing audio. Museroom reads that session to learn the track title, artist, album, artwork, duration and playback position.

Reading it requires notification access. Museroom does not read the contents of your notifications. It counts a fixed list of music apps and nothing else: Spotify, YouTube Music (including the ReVanced and RVX builds), Apple Music, Amazon Music, SoundCloud, Deezer, Tidal, Pandora, JioSaavn, Wynk, Gaana, Anghami, Yandex Music, JOOX, Napster, Qobuz, Bandcamp and Audiomack. Every other app on your device is ignored, and the list only grows when a music app is added to it deliberately.

Browsers, video apps and podcast apps are deliberately not on it. A browser plays whatever the web plays, so counting one would mean recording anything you happen to open.

What is stored on your phone

  • Playback events: play, pause, seek, track change, and a periodic heartbeat.
  • Reconstructed listening sessions and the minutes credited to each.
  • Your sign-in tokens, if you sign in.

If you never sign in, none of this leaves your device.

What is sent to the server

Once you sign in, the app uploads your playback events, your listening sessions and what you are currently playing. This is what makes friends and the leaderboard work. Data is stored in Supabase, hosted on Amazon Web Services.

Signing in with Google gives Museroom your email address and basic profile information. It does not give access to your Gmail, contacts, Drive or any other Google service.

Your email address is never shown to anybody. It is not part of your username, it does not appear on the leaderboard, and it is not displayed anywhere in the app, including to you. Your username is a name you chose.

Your username and picture

New accounts are given a meaningless name, and you can change it to anything not already taken. If you add a profile picture, it is cropped and shrunk on your phone before it is uploaded, and it is stored so that anybody who can see your name can also see it. That includes the public leaderboard. Do not use a picture you would not put on a public page.

Listening rooms

When you listen along with somebody, Museroom plays their track itself, inside the app, using YouTube Music's web player. That player runs in a browser view Museroom keeps hidden, and it behaves like any browser: YouTube can set cookies in it and sees the requests it makes, exactly as it would if you opened the same site yourself.

No audio travels between phones. Each side plays its own copy of the recording, and all that is shared is which track and which moment.

Being in somebody's room is visible to that person. Nobody else is told.

People nearby

This is off unless you switch it on. While it is on, your phone broadcasts a short random code over Bluetooth and listens for others doing the same. The code is not your name, is not derived from your account, changes every fifteen minutes and expires. Only Museroom can match a code to a person, and only while both people have the feature on.

Nothing about your music travels over Bluetooth. Other phones learn only that a code is in range; what you are playing is looked up through this service, subject to the same visibility setting as everything else. Switching it off withdraws your codes immediately, and a private session pauses it.

No location is used, requested or stored. Museroom declares to Android that scan results are never used to work out where you are, which is why it does not ask for location on Android 12 and later.

Who can see your listening

  • What you are playing right now is visible according to your visibility setting: everyone, friends only, or nobody. The default is friends only.
  • Your username, picture and total minutes appear on the public leaderboard if you are opted in. You can opt out and keep using everything else.
  • People physically near you see what you are playing only if you switch that on, and never if your visibility is set to nobody.
  • Your detailed history and event log are visible only to you. This is enforced by database policy, not only by the app.

Deleting your data

You can delete all of your history from within the app, which removes it from this phone and from the server and takes its minutes off your total. Delete my account, under You, goes further: your username, picture, minutes, friends and every row about you are deleted, and the account itself goes with them. Neither can be undone. Removing the app from your phone removes everything stored locally.

Blocking and reporting

Blocking somebody stops them seeing what you play, asking to join your room and sending you a request, and undoes any friendship between you. It is enforced by the database rather than by the app, so it holds however the data is asked for. They are not told, and they cannot see that you have blocked them. Undo it from the You tab.

Reporting somebody records who reported whom, and why, for a person to look at. Nothing happens automatically, and reporting does not block them.

Children

Museroom is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.
