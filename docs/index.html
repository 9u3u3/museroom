Music is better when somebody else can hear it.

Museroom reads whatever is playing on your phone and shows it to the friends you add. It finds the people listening in the room around you, plays what they are playing, and counts every minute.

Who else is in the room

The one nothing else does

Find the people around you, by what they are playing.

Turn on the air and your phone puts a short code out over Bluetooth. Anybody in range running Museroom hears it, and it becomes a face, a name and a song. Tap one and their music becomes yours.

The code changes constantly and means nothing on its own, so nothing about you is readable by anything except Museroom. No location is used, asked for, or stored.

Everything else

Listening rooms

Join what a friend is playing.

Ask, and the moment they say yes their song starts on your phone. When they skip, you skip. There is nothing to press and no other app to open, because Museroom plays it itself. Ad breaks are kept out, since an ad only ever happens to one of you.

Friends

What they are on, right now.

Add somebody by username and their track appears with its artwork and a clock that ticks. You choose who sees yours: everyone, friends only, or nobody. A private session stops recording entirely.

Your name, your face

Anonymous unless you decide otherwise.

Nothing about your account is built out of your email address. Pick a username, add a picture from your gallery if you want one, and that is everything anybody else can see.

What it counts

Spotify and YouTube Music, including the ReVanced and RVX builds of YouTube Music. Nothing else on your device is read. Browsers are deliberately unsupported, because a browser plays whatever the web plays and no permission granted in advance could sensibly cover that.

How it works

  • Android keeps a media session for whichever app is playing audio. Museroom reads the track and its position from there, which is why it works with players that have no public interface of their own.
  • A listening room plays through YouTube Music inside the app. Your friend's track is matched by name, started at the moment they are at, and kept there by nudging the position. No audio travels between phones.
  • The ad slots are removed from the player's data before it reads them, the same way the YouTube Music desktop clients do it.
  • Nearby listeners are found over Bluetooth Low Energy with a rotating token. Museroom declares to Android that scan results are never used to work out where you are, which is true, and means it never asks for location on Android 12 and up.
  • Nothing is shared until you sign in, and what friends can see is yours to set.

Installing it

Museroom is not on the Play Store, so Android puts two warnings in your way. Neither means anything is wrong with the app, and both are explained in full below. Together they take about a minute.

  1. Download the file using the button at the top of this page. Your browser will ask whether to keep it, because it is an app rather than a picture. Keep it.
  2. Open it. Android will say your browser is not allowed to install apps and offer a settings screen. Turn the switch on, come back, and the install continues. You can turn it off again afterwards.
  3. Get past Play Protect. Explained just below.
  4. Give it notification access when the app asks on first launch. If Android calls this a restricted setting, that is explained below too.
Warning 1

"Unsafe app blocked" — Play Protect

Play Protect is Google's scanner. It checks every app being installed against a list of apps Google has seen before, and Museroom is not on that list, because it is downloaded from here rather than from the Play Store. It has not found anything in the app. It is telling you it does not recognise it.

What to tap. The dialog usually has More details and then Install anyway. On some phones the second button is Install without scanning. Either is the same choice.

Why it cannot be turned off. The warning belongs to Google, not to Museroom. Nothing in the app can suppress it, and it will keep appearing for as long as the app is downloaded from a page like this one. The only thing that removes it is publishing through the Play Store.

How to check for yourself, rather than taking that on trust: the whole app is open on GitHub. Every version on this page is built from what is there.

Warning 2

"Restricted setting" — Android 13 and later

Museroom needs notification access. That sounds broader than it is: it is the only way Android lets an app read the media session, which is where the title, artist and position of the current track live. Museroom never reads the contents of a notification, and ignores every app except Spotify and YouTube Music.

On Android 13 and later, that particular switch is greyed out for any app installed outside the Play Store. Tapping it shows Restricted setting and nothing happens. This is Android's decision, taken at the moment the app was installed and based on where it came from. Nothing inside the app can lift it.

What to do. Open Settings → Apps → Museroom, which is the App info page. The app's own Open app info button takes you straight there. Then find Allow restricted settings and turn it on. Where that option sits depends on the phone:

  • Behind the three dots in the top-right corner, on most phones.
  • Under Advanced or More, on some.
  • Near the bottom of the App info page, on others.

If the option is not there at all, it has expired. It only appears for a short while after installing. Uninstall Museroom, install it again, and go to App info within the next few minutes.

You only have to do this once, ever. The permission survives every future update, so install new versions over the top and never uninstall.

If you have a computer, this does the same thing in one line, with the phone plugged in and USB debugging turned on:

adb shell appops set com.museroom.app ACCESS_RESTRICTED_SETTINGS allow
Version 3.8 changes the signing key, once. Every earlier build was signed with a development key, and Android will not install a differently signed app over one it already has. If you have an older Museroom, uninstall it first, then install this. Every version after this one updates in place.

After that, update over the top and never uninstall. Notification access survives an update and does not survive an uninstall, so uninstalling means finding Allow restricted settings all over again.
