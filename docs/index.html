Museroom

See what your friends are listening to, in real time.

Museroom reads whatever is playing on your Android phone and shows it to the friends you add. It counts your listening minutes and ranks them on a leaderboard, and it can find other listeners in the room with you.

Installing

Museroom is not on the Play Store, so Android will ask permission before installing it. Tap the download above, open the file, and allow installs from your browser when prompted.

On first launch the app asks for notification access. That permission is what makes detection possible: Android publishes a media session for whatever is playing, and reading it is the only way to know what you are listening to.

Android 13 and later: "Restricted setting". Android blocks the notification access switch for apps installed outside the Play Store, and nothing in the app can unblock it. Open App info for Museroom and find Allow restricted settings. It is behind the three dots on most phones, under Advanced or More on some, and near the bottom of the page on others.

If the option is missing entirely, uninstall Museroom, install it again, and open App info within the next few minutes. The option only appears for a short while after installing.

You only have to do this once. The setting survives every later update, so long as you install over the top rather than uninstalling first.

If you have a computer with adb, this does the same thing in one line, with USB debugging turned on:

adb shell appops set com.museroom.app ACCESS_RESTRICTED_SETTINGS allow
Play Protect will warn you. Google shows that warning for any app it has not seen before, which means every app that is not on the Play Store. Choosing Install anyway is the whole of it. There is no setting in the app that turns it off, and there is no way to remove it while Museroom is downloaded from here rather than from Google.
Version 3.8 changes the signing key, once. Every earlier build was signed with a development key, and Android will not install a differently signed app over one it already has. Uninstall Museroom first, then install this. It is a one-time step; every version after this updates in place.

Update in place from now on, and never uninstall. Notification access survives an update and does not survive an uninstall, so uninstalling means finding Allow restricted settings all over again.

Listening together

Ask a friend to join what they are playing, and once they say yes Museroom plays it too. It plays it inside Museroom, not by throwing you into another app, so when they skip you skip with them and there is nothing to press. Ad breaks are kept out, because an ad only ever happens to one of you and there is nothing to stay in step with while it runs. You can sign in to YouTube Music from the You tab if you would rather listen on your own account.

What it counts

Spotify and YouTube Music, including the ReVanced and RVX builds of YouTube Music. Nothing else on your device is read. Browsers are deliberately unsupported, because a browser plays whatever the web plays and no permission granted in advance could sensibly cover that.

How it works

  • Android keeps a media session for the app currently playing audio. Museroom reads the track and its position from there, which is why it works with players that have no public interface of their own.
  • A listening room plays through YouTube Music inside the app. Your friend's track is matched by name, then started at the moment they are at, and kept there by nudging the position rather than by streaming any audio between phones.
  • The ad slots are removed from the player's data before it reads them, the same way the YouTube Music desktop clients do it.
  • Minutes are counted when a track finishes, and the totals are worked out on a server rather than taken on trust from the phone.
  • Nothing is shared until you sign in, and what friends can see is yours to set. A private session stops recording entirely.
